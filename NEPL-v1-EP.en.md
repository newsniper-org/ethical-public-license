# NEWSNIPER Ethical Public License, Version 1 — Extension EP (NEPLv1-EP)

**NEWSNIPER Ethical Public License v1 — Extension EP** · draft, first revision (not frozen; wording may change)

**EP stands for Explicit Permission.** That one requirement is what this Extension is.

**This is a translation; the Korean text is authoritative** (clause E8.1).

**This document is an "Extension" to NEPLv1** (NEPLv1 clause 10.4). It does not replace that licence and **has effect only where it is applied together with it.**

This Extension does one thing: **it stops another person's words from being rewritten without their explicit permission.**

---

## E0. About this Extension

E0.1 **Basis.** This Extension is published by the Licence Steward under clause 10.4 of NEPLv1. As that clause provides, this Extension **does not replace NEPLv1**, **has effect only where it is applied together with NEPLv1**, and everything not stated here remains exactly as NEPLv1 provides.

E0.2 **It is optional.** This Extension binds only a work **whose Application Notice states "Extensions: NEPLv1-EP".** Where the Application Notice carries no such entry, this Extension does not exist (NEPLv1 clause 10.4).

E0.3 **This Extension does only two things** — exactly what clause 10.4 of NEPLv1 permits an Extension to do.

  (a) **It removes the making of adaptations from the grant in clause 2.2** — but only as to the "Speaker's Wording" defined in E1.1 below (E2).

  (b) **It adds one condition to the attribution required when that wording is excerpted or rearranged** (E3.4).

E0.4 **This Extension deletes nothing from NEPLv1 and weakens nothing in it.** In particular the **Prohibited Uses in clause 5** are not altered in the slightest by this Extension, as the last sentence of NEPLv1 clause 10.2 already requires.

E0.5 **This Extension grants no new rights whatsoever.** It only takes away. The scope of use of a work carrying this Extension is therefore **always narrower than, or the same as,** that of a work carrying NEPLv1 alone.

E0.6 **Non-binding note.** This Extension brings NEPLv1 closer to a state of "readable but not rewritable". What NEPLv1 says about itself in clause 0.1 therefore applies here too, and more strongly — **it is not "open source" as defined by the OSI, and not "free software" as defined by the FSF.** This clause aids interpretation only and creates no rights or obligations.

---

## E1. Definitions

E1.1 **"The Speaker's Wording"** (below, "that wording") means **those parts which the Application Notice designates as subject to this Extension.** The typical case is a record of what one person said in their own words — a lecture, dictation, interview, consultation, written answer — but it is not confined to that.

  **This Extension does not bind any part the Application Notice has not designated.** Within the same repository, therefore, the Licensor's own prose may be adapted on NEPLv1's ordinary terms.

E1.2 **"The Speaker"** means the person who spoke or wrote that wording, and any successor designated by them in writing under E5.2. **This may be a different person from the Licensor under NEPLv1 clause 1.7** — the person who prepares and distributes a record need not be the person who spoke.

E1.3 **"Altering the wording"** means changing the **words, sentences, order or sense** of that wording. As in NEPLv1 clause 12.1, **differences of layout, line breaks, markup, typeface or file format are not changes to the wording.**

E1.4 **"Correction by juxtaposition"** means recording an error, a supplement or a dissent **in a separate document placed alongside** that wording, without altering it (E5.1).

---

## E2. Narrowing of the grant — adaptations

E2.1 **As to that wording, the making of adaptations and the use of such adaptations is removed from the grant in NEPLv1 clause 2.2.** That is, **rewriting that wording is not permitted at all.**

E2.2 **It may nonetheless be done to the extent the Speaker has expressly permitted in writing** (E4).

E2.3 **Legal character.** The narrowing in E2.1 concerns **the scope of the grant made by clause 2 of NEPLv1.** Distributing that wording in altered form without permission is therefore **a use that was never licensed**, and constitutes **copyright infringement.**

  _(This mirrors NEPLv1 clauses 5.5 and 9.3. Only where the governing law does not recognise it as a limitation of scope, or characterises it as a contractual obligation, does the narrowing subsist as a contractual obligation owed by You to the Speaker and the Licensor.)_

E2.4 **The cure in NEPLv1 clause 8.2 therefore does not apply to this narrowing.** The use lies outside the scope of the grant, so no rights under the licence ever existed and there is nothing to reinstate. _(This is the same position as NEPLv1 clause 8.3 takes for clause 5.)_

E2.5 **This narrowing prevails over the accommodation in NEPLv1 clause 4.** A Rightsholder of the Upstream Work is likewise given no right to alter that wording.

  **The reason is simple** — the additional permission in NEPLv1 clause 4.1 is **granted by the Licensor**, and the Licensor **was never in a position to authorise the adaptation of the Speaker's Wording.** This is the same logic by which clause 6.2 states that the Licensor grants rights only in their own creative contribution.

E2.6 **This Extension does not bind the Software Part.** The grant in NEPLv1 clause 2.1 stands. However, a file designated by the Application Notice as that wording is subject to this Extension regardless of whether it is a Software Part under NEPLv1 clause 1.3.1.

## E3. Outside the reach of this Extension

E3.1 **Quotation has nothing to do with this Extension.** Quotation, criticism, research and reporting within the limits the law of the place of use places on economic rights (e.g. Korean Copyright Act art. 28) **require no permission in the first place**, so this Extension does not narrow them. NEPLv1 clause 6.3 says the same.

E3.2 **Translation is not an alteration of the wording.** Rendering it faithfully into another language is not "altering" within E1.3 and **requires no permission from the Speaker.** The translation permission in NEPLv1 clause 2.2 survives intact.

  A translation shall state that it is a translation and what the original was.

E3.3 **Adaptation into another idiom, however, is an alteration.** Reworking the content to fit another context, audience or medium falls within E2.1 and **requires permission.** The line between a faithful rendering and a fitted one is the line this Extension draws.

E3.4 **Excerpt and rearrangement.** Carrying across only part of that wording is not barred by this Extension, **so long as the wording of the part carried across is not altered.** In that case, however, **the following shall also be shown** — that it is an excerpt, and **where it came from.** _(This is the condition referred to in E0.3(b); it is one line added to the attribution required by NEPLv1 clause 3.1.)_

  _(An excerpt cut so far that the sense changes is not an excerpt but an adaptation. That judgement is made by reference to "sense" in E1.3.)_

E3.5 **Format conversion is not adaptation.** In the same sense as NEPLv1 clause 12.1 provides for the licence text, moving that wording into another document format, encoding or medium is not a change to the wording.

---

## E4. How permission is sought

E4.1 **Permission is obtained in writing** (including electronically). The person seeking it shall state **what they propose to change and how.**

E4.2 **Silence by the Speaker is neither consent nor refusal.** _(This is the same idiom as NEPLv1 clause 1.9(d).)_ While no reply has come, that wording may not be altered — but correction by juxtaposition under E5.1 is always available.

E4.3 **A permission extends only as far as it says.** One permission is not to be read as permitting later, different changes.

E4.4 **A permission creates no conditions beyond this Extension.** In granting it the Speaker may not impose restrictions that NEPLv1 does not provide (NEPLv1 clause 2.5, last sentence).

---

## E5. Where the Speaker cannot be reached

E5.1 **Correction by juxtaposition is always available.** Typographical errors, errors of fact, matters overtaken by time, and dissents are recorded **in a separate document placed alongside, instead of by altering that wording.** That document shall state **who wrote it and when**, and **shall not be presented as the Speaker's words.**

  _(This is what keeps this Extension from becoming a dead end. The wording is locked and **the way to set it right stays open.**)_

E5.2 **Successors.** The Speaker **may designate a successor in writing.** Where they die or become untraceable without such a designation, that wording is dealt with by correction by juxtaposition under E5.1 alone.

E5.3 **And ceasing distribution is always available.** Nothing in this Extension prevents the Licensor from ceasing to distribute that wording. Under NEPLv1 clause 2, however, **the rights of those who lawfully obtained it beforehand are unaffected.**

---

## E6. The Speaker's other rights

E6.1 **This Extension does not displace moral rights.** The rights of attribution and integrity, and equivalent rights, that the Speaker holds under the law of the place of use stand independently of this Extension.

E6.2 **This Extension creates no right of revocation.** The grant in NEPLv1 clause 2 remains **irrevocable**, and this Extension gives nobody a right to take back permissions already granted. **What this Extension does is withhold from the outset, not reclaim what was given.**

E6.3 **This Extension gives the Speaker no exclusive right to interpret this licence.** _(As in NEPLv1 clause 1.9(d).)_

## E7. Application Notice — how to use this Extension

Fill in the **"Extensions" field** of the Application Notice under NEPLv1 clause 12, and add the following.

```
Extensions:        NEPLv1-EP
Extension text:    <path>

Speaker:           <name, or the designation they choose>
                   NB: if they do not wish to be named, record that instead.
Notices to Speaker:
                   <address or e-mail>   (where permission under E4 is sought)
Speaker's successor:
                   <name · address>      (E5.2 — leave blank if none designated)

Subject of this Extension (the Speaker's Wording):
                   <paths or scope>
                   NB: anything not listed here is not bound (E1.1).
```

E7.1 **If no subject is listed, this Extension binds nothing.** It is not a device for locking a whole repository.

E7.2 **No entry in the Application Notice can overturn E0.4.** Clause 5 of NEPLv1 is relaxed neither by this Extension nor by the Application Notice.

## E8. Versions and translations

E8.1 **The Korean text of this Extension is authoritative.** The English, Japanese and German texts are convenience translations; in case of divergence, the Korean text prevails.

E8.2 **Fixed versions.** New versions of this Extension are published **only by the Licence Steward (NEPLv1 clause 1.12).** **Only the version stated in the Application Notice applies; no option such as "or any later version" is provided.**

E8.3 **NEPLv1 clause 10.3 applies to this Extension document as it stands** — it may be copied and distributed verbatim and anyone may apply it to their own work, but **a modified version shall not use the abbreviation "NEPL".** Translating it into another language is permitted and such a translation may bear this name.

E8.4 **Recommendation (non-binding).** In machine-readable metadata, the use of `LicenseRef-NEPL-1.0-EP` is recommended. This is a recommendation; not following it is not a breach (NEPLv1 clauses 12.1 and 12.2).

## E9. Spirit — non-binding postscript

**When you carry across what someone said, you do not carry across the right to change it.** If something in it is wrong, do not correct it in place; write beside it. Then their words remain as they were, and so does the error.

**Better that both remain.**

This clause creates no rights or obligations.
